The coated chip with the pore was mounted in an electrophoresis flow cell. Two reservoirs on each side with a volume of 100 μL (trans and cis) were filled with a filtered and degassed (30 min while being sonicated) buffer of 0.02, 0.14, or 1 M KCl, 10 nM tris-HCl, and 1 nM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (pH 6). A pair of Ag/AgCl pellet electrodes (made from 0.9 mm diameter 99.9% Ag wires. Formed by soaking silver in bleach) were immersed in the two reservoirs and connected to an Axopatch 200B amplifier (Molecular Devices, Inc.) to record ionic current flow through the nanopore (Figure S1). The whole setup was placed in a double Faraday cage to lower the external electrostatic interference. Signals were collected at a 10 kHz sampling rate using a Digidata 1440A (Molecular Devices, Inc.) and filtered at 1 kHz using the built-in low pass Bessel filter of Axopatch.
